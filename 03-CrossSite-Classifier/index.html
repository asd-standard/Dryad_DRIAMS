03 — Cross-Site Classifier

Train on DRIAMS-A (University Hospital Basel), test on B (Canton Basel-Land), C (Canton Aarau), D (Viollier).

Sections

  • Cross-Site Results

Objective

Test whether a model trained on one hospital’s spectra can predict antimicrobial resistance at completely different hospitals. This is the hardest test of generalisation — also known as Cross-site evaluation: different instruments, different patient populations, different bacterial ecology.

Why It Matters

In Federated learning (08), each hospital trains locally and the aggregated model must work across all sites. Cross-site evaluation is the pooled-data analogue — can Site A’s data alone predict B/C/D’s resistance patterns?

Approaches

Logistic Regression: Per-drug L2 regularization LogisticRegression + Threshold Tuning, with and without PCA. See 01 for method details.

MLP: Per-drug regularised MLP + attention MLP, both with Threshold Tuning. See 02 for architecture details.

Preprocessing

  • log1p transform + Standardize to zero mean, fit on A train only

  • No information from B/C/D leaks into preprocessing

  • Split: species-stratified split 80/20 on A; B/C/D used as-is

Key Findings

  • Significant drop in performance compared to Aggregated (pooled) training (01/02): training on a single site’s data limits generalisation

  • MLP handles Domain shift better than LogisticRegression

  • Site D (Viollier, private lab) is consistently the hardest target

  • Cross-site evaluation establishes the lower bound that Federated learning (08) aims to beat by incorporating data from all sites
