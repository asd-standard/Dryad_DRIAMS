  • 07-01-results_dedicated_lr_mlp/results_balacc.csv — Balanced Accuracy per drug × per model

  • 07-01-results_dedicated_lr_mlp/results_auc.csv — AUC-ROC per drug × per model

BalAcc values come from results_balacc.csv, AUC-ROC values from results_auc.csv; RF results are read from rf_results.pkl.

The Random Forest is tuned on the same pooled split, but with a subsampled randomized search rather than the exhaustive grid the LR and MLP families use: 07-03-Dedicated-RF-Fast.ipynb runs RandomizedSearchCV (n_iter=20, cv=2) over n_estimators [100, 200, 300, 500], max_depth [10, 20, 30, None], min_samples_leaf [2, 5, 10] and class_weight [balanced, balanced_subsample], scoring candidates on a 5000-row subsample; the winner is retrained on the full training split and its decision threshold is tuned from 3-fold out-of-fold predictions on that subsample. The main 07 notebook keeps an exhaustive GridSearchCV variant of the same grid for reference — the RF columns above are the fast run’s numbers.

RF Checkpoints

Both artefacts are produced by 07-03 (which writes its own copy under results_dedicated_lr_mlp_rf/); the results file is placed next to the LR/MLP exports so the comparison table can pick it up.

  • results_dedicated_lr_mlp/rf_results.pkl — serialised RF test-set results (BalAcc, AUC, threshold, best params) per drug

  • rf_checkpoints/ — trained RF models per drug (joblib format)

Heatmaps

Model × drug heatmaps provide an at-a-glance comparison of all three model families across all drugs for both Balanced Accuracy and AUC-ROC.

Per-Drug Patterns

  • LR and MLP are effectively tied on average; the MLP leads on Ciprofloxacin, Amoxicillin-Clavulanic acid, Piperacillin-Tazobactam and Amikacin, while LR leads on Gentamicin, Imipenem, Ceftazidime and Vancomycin.

  • RF stays close in AUC but is weaker in Balanced Accuracy; it is the best model on Vancomycin and the weakest on Amikacin, where the few resistant samples make the decision threshold fragile.

  • The class-imbalanced drugs are the least stable across model families and metrics, which is why the aggregated study (08) tracks both BalAcc and AUC.

Threshold Selection

Decision thresholds are selected differently per model family: LR and RF tune the threshold with 3-fold cross-validated predictions within the training split (cross_val_predict), while the MLP tunes it on the external validation split. The per-site worst-case threshold protocol used by the federated clients is documented in 08.

Loss Histories and Model Weights

07 does not persist per-epoch training histories, and MLP weights are not saved (the models/ directory is empty). What is kept: the Random Forest models in rf_checkpoints/ (07-03) and the per-drug result records of the class-weight ablation in results_dedicated_lr_mlp_cw/checkpoints/mlp_cw_<drug>.pkl (metrics, thresholds and best params — not weights). The loss curves for the hyperparameters selected here come from diagnostics that retrain the same configurations:

  • Loss curves for the 07 hyperparameters — pooled best-config reruns (02-01, A+B+C+D) and A-only reruns (03-01), with the implementation caveats.

The reruns use MaldiMLPClassifier rather than 07’s custom PyTorch loop, so the curves are representative of the selected configurations, not bit-identical to the 07 models (whose final training runs up to 100 epochs with cosine T_max=90, a 10-epoch warmup and an internal 10% validation split for early stopping — patience 10, best-validation weights restored). The 8×8 grid is scored on the external validation Balanced Accuracy; the class-weighted CrossEntropyLoss applies only to the 07-02 ablation.

Class Weight Ablation

07-02-Dedicated-MLP-ClassWeight.ipynb explores whether inverse-frequency Class weight improves MLP performance, particularly for drugs with highly imbalanced resistance labels (some drugs have <5% resistant samples). It reuses 07-01’s per-drug splits, preprocessing and LR results (inputs rebuilt by 07-02a), trains the same 8×8 lr×dropout grid with a class-weighted CrossEntropyLoss (per-drug weights = #negatives / #positives from the training labels) and tunes every threshold on the validation split. Run 2026-10-15; artefacts in results_dedicated_lr_mlp_cw/.

On average the weighting is close to neutral — +0.001 Balanced Accuracy (0.784 → 0.785) and +0.006 AUC (0.848 → 0.854) — and the per-drug pattern follows the class balance:

  • Ranking improves almost everywhere: AUC rises on 9 of 10 drugs (largest: Amikacin +0.013, Gentamicin +0.011); the only regression is Vancomycin (−0.004), the drug with the fewest resistant samples (~95).

  • Thresholded accuracy gains on the rarest class: Amikacin +0.024 BalAcc (~85 resistant training samples), Ceftriaxone +0.008, Piperacillin-Tazobactam +0.007, Cefepime +0.002.

  • Costs fall on well-represented drugs: Ciprofloxacin −0.014, Ceftazidime −0.009, Gentamicin −0.003; the remaining three move by ≤0.001.

  • Thresholds are validation-tuned (so the comparison is threshold-fair) and sit far from 0.5 on the imbalanced drugs — e.g. Amikacin 0.09, Ceftriaxone 0.11, Vancomycin 0.78.

  • Even with weighting the MLP (0.785) does not overtake the pooled LR baseline (0.788): inverse-frequency weighting is a small recalibration, not a general accuracy win.
